How it works

You hear the thing. Then you change it. Then you learn its name. Then you use it to build something new.

That's the whole method. Sound first. Jargon after.

Every lesson uses Strudel — a live coding instrument that runs in your browser. You type code, press play, and hear what you wrote. You break it, fix it, and learn the name of the thing you just did.

No instrument required. No DAW required. The browser IS the instrument.

The curriculum

Phase 1 is production: patterns, bass, effects, arrangement, sampling. Phase 2 is harmony: scales, modes, chord theory, voice leading, motivic development. The free tier covers Lessons 0–7 and 8a–8b. All of Phase 1 and the start of Phase 2.

You might be thinking

"I use a DAW, not code"

Good. Keep using it. Strudel patterns export to MIDI. hljod adds a layer of theory understanding underneath your existing workflow. You don't replace your tools — you understand them better.

"8-bit Music Theory is free on YouTube"

8BMT is excellent analysis. But you watch it and close the tab. hljod is the part where you do it. You type the mode, hear the change, break it, fix it. The gap between understanding analysis and being able to compose is what this course bridges.

"Why not just use the Strudel docs?"

Strudel's docs teach Strudel. They'll teach you note("c e g") but not why CEG is a major triad or why Koji Kondo chose it. hljod uses Strudel as the instrument for learning music theory — the theory is the curriculum, the code is the medium.

"Will I stick with it?"

Lessons 0 through 8b are free. No account. No credit card. If the first 10 lessons don't change how you hear music, you haven't lost anything. That's the guarantee.
