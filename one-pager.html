The Problem

Game music inspires millions to want to learn music. But music education ignores game music entirely. Piano teachers are confused when kids ask for "video game music." Producers who make beats can't figure out why game soundtracks sound different. And the 558K people who watch 8-bit Music Theory on YouTube can't interact with anything they learn.

The Solution

hljod is an interactive music theory course where you learn by live-coding recreations of game soundtracks in your browser. No install. No instrument. Type code, hear music, change a note, understand why. The curriculum uses Zelda, Final Fantasy, Undertale, and Persona 5 as the textbook.

The Market
  • Online music education: $3.9-4.6B market, 15% CAGR. Growing fast, driven by self-paced learners.
  • Nobody combines game music + live coding + interactive browser. The intersection is completely unoccupied.
  • Strudel (the tool) is exploding: 441 upvotes on r/livecoding for "my first song." Organic demand, no marketing.
  • Simply Piano makes $200M/yr. Hooktheory charges $149. The middle is empty. Interactive theory at accessible price = open lane.
Why Now
  • Strudel is having its moment -- 300-441 upvote posts, "where do I start?" is a recurring ask across subreddits.
  • Gitori.com launched 5 days ago with interactive theory but NO game music -- validates the market, leaves the niche wide open.
  • 291.4M TikTok views on #videogamemusic. The audience is massive, active, and underserved.
  • The GameStop/nostalgia generation grew up on these soundtracks and now has disposable income.
